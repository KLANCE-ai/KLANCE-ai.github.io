Brown Vs Board of Education

17th of May, 1954

This event was a supreme court descision that made a ruling that disregarded 'seperate but equal'. This ruling allowed schools to become intergerated under the agrument that seperating children based on race was 'unconstitutional'.

Emmett Till's Death

28th of August, 1955

Emmett Till was brutally murdered for alledgely whistling at a white women. This 14 year old boy gained national attention; he is considered the face of the Civil Rights Movement. This is because of his severly battered face that was seen at his open casket funeral.

Montgomery Bus Boycott

5th of December, 1955

Rosa Parks is the face and inspiration for this boycott, her refusal to give up her bus seat remains as one of the biggest thing in the Civil Rights Movement today. This boycott was about African Americans refusing to travel via bus, with Martin Luther King Jr leading the Montgromery Improvement Assossication on the side; his first leadership role in this Movement.

Little Rock 9

4th of September, 1957

Due to the Brown Vs Board of Education decision, schools were intergerated. 9 African American students then enrolled in Little Rock Central High School; experiencing fierce opposition.

Greenboros sit ins

1st of Feburary, 1960

4 young men sat at a Woolworths white only counter after purschasing food, they had asked to be served. When refused, they remained sat there in protest, more students joined and effectively 'paralysed' the business. This form of protest was used frequently after this event.

Freedom Rides

4th of May, 1961

This event included interracial voluteers who traveled on interstate buses into the South. Doing this, they aimed to challenge Jim Crow laws. They faced arrests, violence, and one bus was firebombed.

Birmingham Campaign

3rd of April, 1963

The Birmingham Campaign was considered a last ditch attempt to upstart the Civil Rights Movement. Within this Campaign, there was the Children Crusade that gained national attention and began changing the publics opinion drastically.

March on Washington

28th of August, 1963

March on Washington was a peaceful protest that had over 250,000 partcipants; both white and black people. This protest was for Jobs and Freedom, it also is where the famous 'I have a dream' speech, by King, was said.

Civil Rights Act '64

2nd of July, 1964

Signed by President Lyndon B. Johnson, this act legally outlawed any segeration or discrimination based on race, colour, gender, and religion.

Martin Luther King Jr. Death

4th of April, 1968

Martin Luther King Jr was fatally shot by James Earl Ray, his death affected the movement dramatically. It caused many riots due to racial unrest. It is also considered the end of the Civil Rights Movement.
